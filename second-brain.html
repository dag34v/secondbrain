05 / For the studio team

The partner who has read everything and forgets nothing.

1

Office-hours prep

Two minutes before a founder call: what we said last week, what they committed to, what changed in their KPIs since, which open issues match patterns from other companies. Partner walks in loaded.

2

Pattern surfacing

Co A & Co B both hit a hiring bottleneck at month 6. Co C is at month 5. The brain flags it and pulls what worked for A and B - before the partner asks.

3

Conversation continuity

Fireflies transcripts mean the brain remembers commitments across all four partners. No more "did we tell them yes on the bridge?" The brain knows and links to the timestamp.

4

LP update synthesis

KPI deltas + partner commentary + new risks across the portfolio, into a first-draft LP letter in ten minutes. Edit & send.

5

Knowledge-debt alerts

Pricing playbook hasn't been touched in nine months while four companies asked pricing questions this quarter. Brain pings the playbook owner.

6

Diligence acceleration

Market sizing, competitive map, GTM scaffold for a new idea, drawn from prior memos in the same vertical. Two days of work into an afternoon.

Pilot order --> ship #1 first (highest frequency, partners use it daily). #2 second (the demo that wins partners over). #4 third (the LP-facing win that justifies the build out loud).

06 / For the founder

The 34V partner who is on call at 11pm, with the studio's full memory behind every answer.

1

"How did Co B handle this?"

Churn spike, payroll in Morocco, switching ad platforms. Founder asks; brain returns the actual answer from a sibling company - with consent gates and the partner who handled it cc'd.

2

Investor-update drafting

Founder triggers it. Brain pulls metrics, drafts the narrative in their voice, flags what investors usually ask. Fifteen minutes instead of two hours.

3

Sales-call review

Founder forwards a Fireflies link. Brain: "buyer hesitated at minute 18 on pricing; matches three deals Co C closed by switching to annual prepay; here's the email Co C sent."

4

Reading the room

"I have my quarterly with Partner X on Friday." Brain returns the three points they pushed on last time, their view on this question from prior transcripts, and the version of the update most likely to land.

5

Hiring shortlist

"I need a fractional CFO with North Africa SaaS experience." Brain returns names the studio has surfaced or worked with before, with warm-intro drafts.

6

Plan vs reality

The brain remembers the founder's own 90-day plan. Each week: "you said X by week 6; you're at week 7 and X isn't done. Re-plan or escalate?"

What this beats: "go use ChatGPT". A founder with ChatGPT has the world's knowledge. They don't have the other founders in our portfolio. That's the one thing we can offer that no AI vendor can.

07 / The trust contract

Get "what of mine is visible to whom?" right before "what can the bot do for me?"

The first time Co C sees an answer that came from Co A, the question every founder asks is the same: did Co A consent? Did the partner approve the cross? Was anything identifying leaked? If we get that wrong on day one, founders close the tab and never open it again.

1

Verbatim with attribution

The actual answer from Co A, with their name on it. Requires explicit per-question opt-in from Co A.

2

Anonymised pattern

"A sibling company in your stage hit this and resolved it by X." No identifying detail. Default for most surfaces.

3

Studio-internal only

Pattern surfaces to partners, never to founders. For anything sensitive enough that founders shouldn't see it.

Three tiers in week one. Founders pick the tier per data type. Partners reserve the right to refuse to surface anything - not every pattern that would compound value should be surfaced. Partner judgment is the gate.

08 / The pattern engine

The non-obvious value sits between the use cases.

Pattern
engine
Co A
Solved hiring bottleneck at M6
Co B
Same shape, fixed with channel mix
Co C
M5, about to hit it

push, not pull - the brain alerts the partner before Co C asks

Indexed by problem shape, not by keyword.

"Hiring a senior salesperson in a market with thin SaaS talent" and "filling a head-of-revenue role in Casablanca" are the same pattern. The brain knows that. Partner corrections back-propagate: when a partner says "the North Africa answer is X, not the default", the next founder asking gets X.

Standard Metrics will tell you Co C's CAC is rising. Only the brain knows Co A and Co B saw the same shape and fixed it. The conversation is where the pattern lives - and the monitoring tools don't see the conversation.

ResearchGate, network-based VC success analysis: 67% of predictive power comes from network features (84.7% AUC). Directionally relevant - the network is the asset; the pattern engine makes it legible.

The highest-value feature is also the highest-risk. Build the consent UX (previous slide) before the alerting UX.

09 / The dial

Start advisory. Earn the right to operational.

Autonomy is a dial, not a switch. We dial up task by task as trust and data justify it.

Advisoryv1 - day one
Brain proposes · human chooses · nothing touches the world
Assistedafter 30 days
Brain executes on a single click - schedule a meeting, send an intro, log an action item
Operationalearned per task
Brain runs categories of work without per-action review

A task only promotes when all four are true:

  1. High volumeRepeats enough that automation pays back.
  2. ReversibleMistakes are undoable in minutes.
  3. AuditableEvery action carries a who/why/source trail.
  4. Trust-testedTwo founders ran advisory mode for 30 days, zero overrides.

Atlan: only 21% of enterprises have mature governance for autonomous agents. AURA framework (arXiv 2510.15739) maps liability directly to delegated authority. One unrecoverable mistake across the portfolio kills founder trust faster than any feature wins it back.

11 / How we own this

Build internal first. Instrument the data. Keep spinout optionality.

Three structural options. I think A now, with B & C kept open.

Recommend
Option A

Internal IP

Tool built and used inside 34V. Not sold.

Fastest. Tightest feedback loop. Compounds straight into portfolio returns. No GTM tax.

Downside: no outside revenue. Stays a cost center until we prove it.

Option B

Spinout

Productise & sell to other studios and VCs.

Real revenue. External validation.

But: portfolio-mgmt software is $3.94B in 2026, 11.3% CAGR (Grand View), with funded incumbents. Slack, Basecamp, Asana all began internal & spun out - second act, not first.

Option C

Studio-of-studios

License the playbook & tooling to other regional studios.

Capital-light. Plays the Morocco-to-global angle.

But: requires a product already proven against our own portfolio. C is the bet B unlocks - not the starting point.

Keeping optionality means three concrete things, none of them expensive if we do them now:
  1. Multi-tenant data model from day one, with 34V as the only tenant.
  2. Per-skill provenance & feedback capture, exportable.
  3. IP assignment terms in employment contracts so a future entity can receive the work.

12 / What kills this

The risks are human. They're not technical.

1

Founders don't adopt

They keep using the group chat with the partner. The brain sits in a tab nobody clicks. Shelfware.

63% of orgs cite underutilised apps as a top consolidation driver. 25-30% of IT spend wasted (Integrate.io).
2

Playbooks too idiosyncratic

Every company turns out to be a special case. The pattern engine never lands because there are no patterns.

The one I'd watch hardest. We won't know until v1 ships and we try to surface the first cross-company alert.
3

Nobody owns maintenance

Pricing playbook is fine in month 1, stale in month 9, actively wrong in month 18. Brain gives confident wrong answers.

~70% of digital transformations miss objectives (MeltingSpot). 42%+ of failures trace to change management (Sci-Tech Today).

"Will it hallucinate?" is the wrong worry. That's a 2024 question. The 2026 questions are whether a founder opens it on a Tuesday morning, and whether the playbook is still true six months later.

What we do about it --> embed the brain inside existing rituals (office hours, founder check-ins, IC prep). Name one playbook owner per skill. Define adoption KPIs before launch - weekly active partners, weekly active founders, correction-rate trend.
