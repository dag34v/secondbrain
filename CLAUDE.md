# 34V Second Brain - Deck Conventions

How the partner deck (`second-brain.html`) gets written.

## Voice

Write like a senior operator thinking alongside peers, not presenting to a
hierarchy. The reader is a partner, not an audience. The goal is to sound
like a smart, well-read person writing quickly to someone they respect.

- Short sentences. Simple words. No jargon. No AI-sounding text.
- Remove filler, adverbs, adjectives unless essential.
- Active voice. Positive phrasing over negatives.
- Specific data, not vague descriptions. "Revenue grew 13% ($230MM)",
  not "grew significantly".
- Lead with context or framing, then the conclusion. Not the reverse.
- One idea per paragraph.
- Mix paragraphs (for argument-building) with numbered lists (for
  priorities). Never both at once.
- Informal connectors are fine: "&", "-->", "I think", "I totally agree".
  Use them where they fit the rhythm, not for decoration.
- Reference companies, funds, people, data points inline as evidence.
- Leave productive tension. Not every point needs resolution.
- Use simple dashes ("-") if needed. No em-dashes or en-dashes.

## What to avoid

- Bold headers inside a slide's body.
- Block labels like "CLAIM / EVIDENCE / WHY / DO" wrapping every section.
- Excessive bullets. If three points flow as a paragraph, write them as
  a paragraph.
- Corporate language: "leverage", "synergies", "in today's world",
  "in the age of AI", "unlock value".
- Sentence-level hedging that makes the writer sound junior.

## Slide structure

Each slide carries one idea. The idea sits in the title. The body
defends it as prose, with one numbered list at most. Citations are
parenthetical and inline ("Affinity 2026", "Grand View"). Full sources
resolve in `sources.md`.

Speaker notes at the bottom of each slide are for partners running
the session. They carry caveats, source flags, and what to pressure-test.

## Citations

- Every external fact carries a parenthetical reference in the prose.
- `sources.md` lists every reference with the URL.
- If a figure cannot be verified, say so. Do not invent.
